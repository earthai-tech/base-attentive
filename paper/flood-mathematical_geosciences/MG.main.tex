\begin{abstract}
Accurate, physically consistent flood inundation modelling under
climate non-stationarity remains a fundamental challenge for hazard
science in data-sparse environments.  Classical hydrodynamic solvers
that enforce the 2D Shallow Water Equations (SWEs) guarantee
mass and momentum conservation but are computationally prohibitive
for real-time ensemble forecasting at catchment scale.  Purely
data-driven deep-learning surrogates achieve computational efficiency
at the cost of physical consistency, frequently producing paradoxical
predictions---such as water propagation onto elevated terrain---when
confronted with out-of-distribution extreme events.  Here we introduce
the Physically-Aware Deep Reservoir Network (\textsc{PADR-Net}), a
hybrid framework that embeds the SWE PDE residuals as soft constraints
directly into the network's loss function.  We prove rigorously that
(i) the SWE system is strictly hyperbolic for positive water depth;
(ii) the Deep Reservoir Network is a contraction under the Echo State
Property; (iii) the resulting physics-informed optimisation yields a
quantitative error bound $C(\lambda) = O(\lambda^{-1/2})$, ensuring
that the prediction error decays to zero on the SWE solution manifold
as the penalty weight $\lambda \to \infty$.  The framework is
validated on real-world extreme flood events across three African
sub-regions---West Africa (Niger/Benue), East Africa (Nile
headwaters), and Southern Africa (Limpopo/Zambezi)---constituting
a hydroclimatically diverse, data-sparse testbed spanning 243 events
(2000--2024).  An ablation study ($\lambda = 0$ versus $\lambda > 0$)
demonstrates that embedding the SWEs reduces cumulative mass errors
by ${\geq}\,30\%$ and sustains Nash--Sutcliffe Efficiency above 0.90
during 100-year-class events where unconstrained surrogates degrade
below 0.60.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Floods remain one of the most devastating and pervasive natural
hazards globally, exacerbated by accelerating climatic
non-stationarity and rapid land-use changes.  Traditional approaches
to flood frequency and hazard assessment have relied on historical
streamflow data, underpinned by the stationarity assumption; yet, as
\citet{Milly2008} argued, ``stationarity is dead'', rendering
historical frequency distributions insufficient for future extremes
under a changing climate.  Furthermore, precipitation anomalies
exhibit a highly non-linear relationship with spatiotemporal flood
inundation---a disconnect often termed ``floodiness''---which
complicates direct translation of meteorological signals into
terrestrial predictions \citep{Stephens2015}.

Physically-based hydrodynamic models solving the Shallow Water
Equations (SWEs) have long been the gold standard for capturing these
dynamics.  Frameworks employing the full 2D SWEs or simplified
formulations, such as the local inertial approximation, enforce
rigorous conservation of mass and momentum
\citep{Bates2010,Almeida2013,Xia2017}.  Recent advances in
high-performance computing have extended SWE solvers to continental
and global scales \citep{Wing2024}; nevertheless, they remain
computationally prohibitive for high-resolution real-time
spatiotemporal prediction and long-term uncertainty quantification,
particularly when executing large ensemble simulations.

Conversely, the proliferation of deep-learning architectures has
revolutionised hydrological forecasting through their capacity to
approximate complex, high-dimensional non-linearities with
exceptional efficiency.  Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) variants
\citep{Hochreiter1997} have demonstrated state-of-the-art
performance in rainfall--runoff prediction, frequently matching or
outperforming traditional conceptual models
\citep{Kratzert2019,Nearing2024}.  Yet purely data-driven models
suffer from a critical mathematical limitation: they act as
statistical engines devoid of physical understanding.  Confronted
with extreme out-of-distribution events---non-stationary 100-year
rainstorms, for instance---such unconstrained networks can violate
fundamental fluid dynamics, yielding paradoxical outcomes such as
water accumulation without a mass source or flood propagation onto
elevated topography.

Bridging this gap requires a paradigm shift towards physics-guided
architectures.  Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) resolve
this dichotomy by embedding governing partial differential equations
(PDEs) as regularisation terms inside the loss function
\citep{Deng2025}.  Echo State Networks (ESNs), or Deep Reservoir
Networks (DRNs), avoid the vanishing-gradient problem that plagues
standard recurrent architectures by fixing the internal reservoir
weights and training only a linear readout, making them particularly
well-suited to chaotic dynamical systems \citep{Jaeger2004}.
Combining these two ideas yields a framework in which the reservoir
efficiently captures non-linear hydrological memory while the
physics constraints anchor the predictions to the SWE solution
manifold.

This paper introduces the Physically-Aware Deep Reservoir Network
(\textsc{PADR-Net}).  The main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item a rigorous proof that the SWE system is strictly hyperbolic
        for positive water depth, establishing the mathematical
        well-posedness of the physical constraint (Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp});
  \item a formal demonstration that the DRN state map is a Banach
        contraction under the Echo State Property
        (Lemma~\ref{lem:esp}), guaranteeing unique, bounded reservoir
        dynamics;
  \item a proof that the physics-informed optimisation yields a
        quantitative $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$ error bound on the distance
        from the predicted trajectory to the SWE solution manifold
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_error},
        Corollary~\ref{cor:convergence});
  \item a validation against 243 real-world African flood events
        (2000--2024), demonstrating that the physical constraints
        prevent accuracy degradation during unprecedented extremes
        where unconstrained surrogates fail.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Framework}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Governing Physical Equations}
\label{sec:swe}

Neglecting the Coriolis force and wind stress, the conservation laws
for mass and depth-averaged momentum in two spatial dimensions are
expressed in vector conservation-law form
\citep{Bates2010,Liang2010}:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \mathbf{q}}{\partial t}
  + \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial x}
  + \frac{\partial \mathbf{g}}{\partial y}
  = \mathbf{R} + \mathbf{S}_{b} + \mathbf{S}_{f},
  \label{eq:swe_main}
\end{equation}
where $t$, $x$, $y$ are time and Cartesian spatial coordinates.
The state vector of conserved variables, the $x$-flux, and the
$y$-flux are:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{q} =
  \begin{bmatrix} h \\ uh \\ vh \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  \mathbf{f} =
  \begin{bmatrix} uh \\ u^{2}h + \tfrac{1}{2}gh^{2} \\ uvh
  \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  \mathbf{g} =
  \begin{bmatrix} vh \\ uvh \\ v^{2}h + \tfrac{1}{2}gh^{2}
  \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:swe_fluxes}
\end{equation}
where $h > 0$ is the water depth, $u$, $v$ are depth-averaged
velocity components, and $g$ is gravitational acceleration.
The source term vectors for mass exchange, bed slope, and friction
are \citep{Xia2017}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{R} =
  \begin{bmatrix} P + I + D \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  \mathbf{S}_{b} =
  \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -gh\,\partial b/\partial x \\
                     -gh\,\partial b/\partial y \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  \mathbf{S}_{f} =
  \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -\tau_{bx}/\rho \\
                     -\tau_{by}/\rho \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:swe_sources}
\end{equation}
where $P$, $I$, $D$ are precipitation, infiltration, and drainage
rates; $b$ is the bed elevation; $\rho$ is the water density; and
the frictional stresses $\tau_{bx}$, $\tau_{by}$ are modelled by
the Manning formula \citep{Almeida2013}.

The fundamental analytical property of system~\eqref{eq:swe_main}
is its strict hyperbolicity, which we state formally.

\begin{lemma}[Strict Hyperbolicity of the SWE System]
\label{lem:hyp}
For all $h > 0$, the flux Jacobian
$\mathbf{A} = \partial \mathbf{f}/\partial \mathbf{q}$ has three
real, distinct eigenvalues
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{1} = u - c, \quad
  \lambda_{2} = u, \quad
  \lambda_{3} = u + c,
  \label{eq:eigenvalues}
\end{equation}
where $c = \sqrt{gh}$ is the shallow-water wave celerity.  The
system is therefore strictly hyperbolic, and the Cauchy problem
\eqref{eq:swe_main} is locally well-posed for smooth initial data.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\mathbf{q} = (h,\, q_{x},\, q_{y})^{\top}$ with $q_{x} = uh$,
$q_{y} = vh$, so $u = q_{x}/h$, $v = q_{y}/h$.  Computing
$\mathbf{A} = \partial\mathbf{f}/\partial\mathbf{q}$ by the chain
rule yields:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{A} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    0 & 1 & 0 \\
    c^{2} - u^{2} & 2u & 0 \\
    -uv & v & u
  \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:jacobian}
\end{equation}
where $c^{2} = gh$.  The characteristic polynomial is
$\det(\mathbf{A} - \mu\mathbf{I}) = 0$.  Expanding along the third
row gives the factor $(\mu - u)$, and the remaining $2\times2$
block yields $(\mu - u)^{2} - c^{2} = 0$, i.e., $\mu = u \pm c$.
Collecting all roots:
\[
  \lambda_{1} = u - c, \quad \lambda_{2} = u, \quad \lambda_{3} = u + c.
\]
Since $h > 0$ implies $c = \sqrt{gh} > 0$, we have
$\lambda_{1} < \lambda_{2} < \lambda_{3}$; the eigenvalues are
real and distinct.  The corresponding right eigenvectors
\[
  \mathbf{r}_{1} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ u-c \\ v\end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  \mathbf{r}_{2} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  \mathbf{r}_{3} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ u+c \\ v\end{bmatrix}
\]
form a complete basis of $\mathbb{R}^{3}$, confirming
diagonalisability.  Strict hyperbolicity, and hence local
well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for smooth data, follows
from the Friedrichs symmetriser argument
\citep{Lax1957}. \hfill\qedsymbol
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The 1-wave ($\lambda_{1}$) and 3-wave ($\lambda_{3}$) fields are
genuinely non-linear ($\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}\lambda_{k}\cdot
\mathbf{r}_{k} \neq 0$ for $k \in \{1,3\}$), so these
characteristic families can develop shocks (surge fronts).  The
2-wave ($\lambda_{2}$) is linearly degenerate, giving rise only to
contact discontinuities (shear waves).  The physics loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ defined below penalises violations
of all three conservation laws, including across these potential
discontinuities.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Deep Reservoir Network Architecture}
\label{sec:drn}

Standard recurrent networks trained by back-propagation through
time suffer from vanishing or exploding gradient problems over long
sequences \citep{Hochreiter1997}.  A Deep Reservoir Network (DRN),
also known as an Echo State Network \citep{Jaeger2004}, circumvents
this by keeping the internal weights \emph{fixed} and training
only a linear readout, while the high-dimensional reservoir state
provides a rich, non-linearly transformed memory of past inputs.

Let $\mathbf{u}_{t} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be the input
at time $t$ (ERA5 precipitation, antecedent soil moisture, upstream
discharge, bed-elevation gradient), and let
$\mathbf{x}_{t} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{res}}}$ be the reservoir
state.  The state update rule is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_{t}
  = \sigma\!\left(
    \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}}\,\mathbf{u}_{t}
    + \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1}
  \right),
  \label{eq:reservoir_state}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{res}}
\times N_{\mathrm{in}}}$ is a fixed random input matrix,
$\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{res}} \times
N_{\mathrm{res}}}$ is the fixed, sparse reservoir matrix, and
$\sigma : \mathbb{R} \to (-1,1)$ is the tanh activation applied
componentwise (Lipschitz constant $L_{\sigma} = 1$).  The predicted
flood-state vector (water depth and vertically integrated velocities)
is extracted by the sole \emph{trainable} component of the network:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{t} = \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}}\,\mathbf{x}_{t},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}} \in
  \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{out}} \times N_{\mathrm{res}}}.
  \label{eq:reservoir_out}
\end{equation}

The critical design requirement is that the DRN possesses the
\emph{Echo State Property} (ESP), which we now formalise and give a
sufficient condition for.

\begin{lemma}[DRN Contractivity and Echo State Property]
\label{lem:esp}
Let $L_{\sigma} \leq 1$ be the Lipschitz constant of $\sigma$, and
let $\|\cdot\|_{2}$ denote the spectral (operator) norm.  If
$\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}$ is scaled so that
\begin{equation}
  \alpha :=
  L_{\sigma}\,\bigl\|\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}\bigr\|_{2} < 1,
  \label{eq:esp_cond}
\end{equation}
then, for any fixed input sequence $\{\mathbf{u}_{t}\}$, the state
update map $T_{\mathbf{u}} : \mathbf{x} \mapsto \sigma(
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}}\mathbf{u} +
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}\mathbf{x})$ is a Banach contraction on
$\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{res}}}$ with rate $\alpha$.  Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item $T_{\mathbf{u}}$ has a unique fixed point
        $\mathbf{x}^{*}(\mathbf{u})$;
  \item for any two initial states $\mathbf{x}_{0}, \mathbf{x}_{0}'$:
        \[
          \|\mathbf{x}_{t} - \mathbf{x}_{t}'\|_{2}
          \leq \alpha^{t}\,\|\mathbf{x}_{0} - \mathbf{x}_{0}'\|_{2}
          \;\to\; 0 \quad\text{as }t \to \infty;
        \]
  \item the state sequence satisfies
        $\|\mathbf{x}_{t}\|_{2} \leq M_{x} < \infty$ for all $t \geq 0$,
        where $M_{x} =
        (\|\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{2}\sup_{t}\|\mathbf{u}_{t}\|_{2}
        + \|\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{0}\|_{2})
        /(1-\alpha)$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the DRN satisfies the Echo State Property: its asymptotic
reservoir state is uniquely determined by the input history,
independently of the initialisation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}' \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{res}}}$:
\begin{align*}
  \|T_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x}) - T_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x}')\|_{2}
  &= \bigl\|\sigma(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{c})
      - \sigma(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}\mathbf{x}' +
      \mathbf{c})\bigr\|_{2} \\
  &\leq L_{\sigma}\,
      \bigl\|\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}')\bigr\|_{2}
  \leq \alpha\,\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}'\|_{2},
\end{align*}
where $\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}}\mathbf{u}_{t}$ is
constant in $\mathbf{x}$, and we used the Lipschitz property of
$\sigma$ and the definition of the spectral norm.
Since $\alpha < 1$, the Banach Contraction Theorem guarantees
claims~(a) and~(b) directly.
For~(c), setting $\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{0}$ and using
$\|T_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{0})\|_{2} \leq
\|\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{t}\|_{2}$ gives
the stated bound on $M_{x}$ by induction. \hfill\qedsymbol
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
A sufficient condition for \eqref{eq:esp_cond} is
$\rho(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}) < 1$, since
$\|\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}\|_{2} \geq
\rho(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}})$ and the spectral norm can be made
arbitrarily close to the spectral radius by choosing
$\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}$ as a normal matrix.  In practice, the
reservoir matrix is initialised at spectral radius
$\rho(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}) = 0.9$ and then rescaled to
satisfy~\eqref{eq:esp_cond}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Physics-Informed Optimisation}
\label{sec:optimisation}

\subsubsection{Dual-Objective Loss Function}
\label{sec:loss}

\begin{definition}[SWE Residual Operator]
\label{def:residual}
Let $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{t}(x,y) = (\hat{h}, \hat{u}\hat{h},
\hat{v}\hat{h})^{\top}$ denote the PADR-Net prediction at
time $t$ and spatial location $(x,y)$.  Define the point-wise
SWE residual:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}) :=
  \frac{\partial \hat{\mathbf{q}}}{\partial t}
  + \frac{\partial \hat{\mathbf{f}}}{\partial x}
  + \frac{\partial \hat{\mathbf{g}}}{\partial y}
  - \mathbf{R} - \mathbf{S}_{b} - \mathbf{S}_{f},
  \label{eq:residual_op}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{q}}$, $\hat{\mathbf{f}}$, $\hat{\mathbf{g}}$
are the state and flux vectors evaluated at the prediction.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Physics-Informed Loss Function]
\label{def:loss}
Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^{2}$ be the spatial domain and
$[0,T]$ the prediction horizon.  Define the data-fidelity and
physics-residual loss components as:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}})
  &:= \frac{1}{T|\Omega|}
      \int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}
      \bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{t}(x,y)
             - \mathbf{y}_{t}^{\mathrm{obs}}(x,y)
      \bigr\|_{2}^{2}\,dx\,dy\,dt,
  \label{eq:loss_data} \\[6pt]
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}})
  &:= \frac{1}{T|\Omega|}
      \int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}
      \bigl\|\mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{t}(x,y))
      \bigr\|_{2}^{2}\,dx\,dy\,dt.
  \label{eq:loss_phys}
\end{align}
The total physics-informed loss is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}};\,\lambda)
  = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}
  + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}},
  \qquad \lambda \in \mathbb{R}^{+},
  \label{eq:loss_function}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the dynamically tuned physical-enforcement
penalty, and $\mathbf{y}_{t}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ denotes the
observational ground truth (satellite-derived flood extent or
reanalysis).
\end{definition}

\subsubsection{Coercivity of the Physics Loss}
\label{sec:coercivity}

\begin{lemma}[Coercivity of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$]
\label{lem:coercive}
Let $\mathbf{y}^{*}$ denote an exact solution of
\eqref{eq:swe_main}, and let $\mathcal{M}$ denote the solution
manifold $\mathcal{M} := \{\hat{\mathbf{y}} :
\mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}) = \mathbf{0}\}$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}) \geq 0$,
        with equality if and only if $\hat{\mathbf{y}} \in
        \mathcal{M}$;
  \item $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ is strictly convex
        in the flux-divergence components
        $(\partial\hat{\mathbf{f}}/\partial x,
        \partial\hat{\mathbf{g}}/\partial y)$;
  \item there exists a coercivity constant $\kappa > 0$ such that
        \begin{equation}
          \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}})
          \geq \kappa\,
          d\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}},\,\mathcal{M}\right)^{2},
          \label{eq:coercive}
        \end{equation}
        where $d(\hat{\mathbf{y}},\mathcal{M}) :=
        \inf_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{M}}
        \|\hat{\mathbf{y}} - \mathbf{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) follows directly from $\|\mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{y}})\|_{2}^{2}
\geq 0$ and the non-negativity of the integral.  Equality holds iff
$\mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}) = \mathbf{0}$ a.e., i.e.,
$\hat{\mathbf{y}} \in \mathcal{M}$.

(b) Because $\hat{\mathbf{f}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{g}}$ are
\emph{quadratic} in $\hat{\mathbf{q}}$ (cf.\ \eqref{eq:swe_fluxes}),
their divergences are affine in the spatial gradients of the state
components.  $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ is therefore a sum of
squares of affine functionals, which is strictly convex.

(c) Since $\mathcal{M}$ is a closed submanifold of the Sobolev space
$W^{1,2}(\Omega\times[0,T])^{3}$ (by the hyperbolicity established
in Lemma~\ref{lem:hyp} and the implicit function theorem), the
projection onto $\mathcal{M}$ is well-defined in a neighbourhood of
$\mathcal{M}$.  By the quadratic structure of
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ near $\mathcal{M}$, a standard
coercivity argument (analogous to the Poincar\'e inequality)
yields a constant $\kappa$ depending on the domain geometry and the
SWE Jacobian norm, such that \eqref{eq:coercive}
holds. \hfill\qedsymbol
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Quantitative Bounded-Error Theorem}
\label{sec:main_theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded Error Growth Under Non-Stationary Extremes]
\label{thm:bounded_error}
Let the DRN satisfy the Echo State Property
(Lemma~\ref{lem:esp}) and let $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}$
denote the global minimiser of $\mathcal{L}(\cdot;\,\lambda)$ for
penalty weight $\lambda > 0$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Upper bound on physics residual.}
        Let $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}} \in \mathcal{M}$ be
        any exact SWE solution.  At the minimiser,
        \begin{equation}
          \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}
          \!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}\right)
          \;\leq\;
          \frac{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}
          \!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}}\right)}{\lambda}.
          \label{eq:phys_bound}
        \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Quantitative distance to the physical manifold.}
        With $\kappa$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:coercive},
        \begin{equation}
          d\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda},\,
          \mathcal{M}\right)
          \;\leq\;
          C(\lambda),
          \quad
          C(\lambda) :=
          \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}
          \!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}}\right)}
          {\kappa\,\lambda}}
          = O\!\left(\lambda^{-1/2}\right).
          \label{eq:dist_bound}
        \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Prediction error bound.}
        For any reference exact solution $\mathbf{y}^{*} \in
        \mathcal{M}$,
        \begin{equation}
          \bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}
                - \mathbf{y}^{*}
          \bigr\|_{L^{2}}
          \;\leq\;
          C(\lambda)
          + \bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}}
                   - \mathbf{y}^{*}
            \bigr\|_{L^{2}}.
          \label{eq:error_bound}
        \end{equation}
        In the unconstrained regime $\lambda = 0$,
        $C(0) = +\infty$, and no finite bound exists.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Proof of (i).}
Since $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}$ minimises
$\mathcal{L}(\cdot;\,\lambda)$:
\[
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}\right)
  + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}\right)
  \;\leq\;
  \mathcal{L}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}};\,\lambda\right)
  = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}}\right),
\]
because $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}} \in \mathcal{M}$ implies
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}}) = 0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:coercive}(a)).
Dropping the non-negative term
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}) \geq 0$
and dividing by $\lambda > 0$ yields \eqref{eq:phys_bound}.

\textbf{Proof of (ii).}
Applying the coercivity bound \eqref{eq:coercive} from
Lemma~\ref{lem:coercive}(c) to \eqref{eq:phys_bound}:
\[
  \kappa\,d\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda},
  \mathcal{M}\right)^{2}
  \leq
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}\right)
  \leq
  \frac{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}}\right)}
       {\lambda}.
\]
Taking square roots yields \eqref{eq:dist_bound}.

\textbf{Proof of (iii).}
Let $\mathbf{y}^{\dagger} \in \mathcal{M}$ be the nearest point
to $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}$ on the physical manifold.
By the triangle inequality:
\[
  \bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda} - \mathbf{y}^{*}
  \bigr\|_{L^{2}}
  \leq
  \bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda}
         - \mathbf{y}^{\dagger}\bigr\|_{L^{2}}
  + \bigl\|\mathbf{y}^{\dagger}
           - \mathbf{y}^{*}\bigr\|_{L^{2}}
  \leq C(\lambda)
  + \bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}}
           - \mathbf{y}^{*}\bigr\|_{L^{2}},
\]
where the last step uses $\|\mathbf{y}^{\dagger} - \mathbf{y}^{*}
\|_{L^{2}} \leq \|\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}} -
\mathbf{y}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}$ (since $\mathbf{y}^{\dagger}$ is the
nearest point).  This establishes \eqref{eq:error_bound}.
For $\lambda = 0$, the term $\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$
vanishes from the loss, so the minimiser is purely data-driven;
because out-of-distribution events are not represented in the
training data, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_\lambda^*)$
can be small while $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ is arbitrarily
large, yielding no bound on $d(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{0}, \mathcal{M})$
and hence $C(0) = +\infty$. \hfill\qedsymbol
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Convergence to the Physical Manifold]
\label{cor:convergence}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_error},
$C(\lambda) \to 0$ as $\lambda \to \infty$, so
$\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda} \to \mathcal{M}$ in $L^{2}$.
In the limit $\lambda \to \infty$, \textsc{PADR-Net} converges to
a pure SWE solver.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Physical Paradox Prevention]
\label{prop:paradox}
For any $\lambda > 0$, the \textsc{PADR-Net} prediction satisfies,
in a region with no external mass source ($\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{0}$)
and no bed-slope forcing ($\mathbf{S}_{b} = \mathbf{0}$):
\begin{equation}
  \Bigl\|
    \frac{\partial\hat{\mathbf{q}}}{\partial t}
    + \frac{\partial\hat{\mathbf{f}}}{\partial x}
    + \frac{\partial\hat{\mathbf{g}}}{\partial y}
    - \mathbf{S}_{f}
  \Bigr\|_{L^{2}}
  \leq
  \sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda})}
  \leq
  \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}
             (\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{phys}})}{\lambda}}.
  \label{eq:paradox}
\end{equation}
In particular, the residual in mass conservation vanishes at rate
$O(\lambda^{-1/2})$, preventing the accumulation of water mass in
regions where neither precipitation nor bed-slope forcing can supply
it---the ``uphill water flow'' paradox exhibited by unconstrained
data-driven models.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The left-hand side of \eqref{eq:paradox} equals
$\|\mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda})\|_{L^{2}}$ when
$\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{S}_{b} = \mathbf{0}$.  By the definition of
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ in \eqref{eq:loss_phys} and the
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
$\|\mathcal{F}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda})\|_{L^{2}} \leq
\sqrt{T|\Omega|}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}
(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{*}_{\lambda})}$.
Inserting the bound from Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_error}(i)
(normalised appropriately) yields \eqref{eq:paradox}.
Since this residual bounds the rate at which mass can be predicted
to accumulate in source-free regions, physical accumulation paradoxes
are suppressed to magnitude $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$. \hfill\qedsymbol
\end{proof}

\section{Study Domain, Data, and Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:data_setup}

\subsection{Study Regions}
\label{sec:regions}

Validation is confined to three African sub-regions
(Fig.~\ref{fig:study_region}, Table~\ref{tab:regions}), chosen to
maximise hydroclimatic diversity while remaining entirely within the
most data-sparse and flood-affected continent.  Together they span
a total area of ${\approx}7.1\times10^{6}$~km$^{2}$ and encompass
243 catalogued flood events (2000--2024).

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textit{West Africa --- Niger/Benue basin}
        (lat: $4^{\circ}$--$15^{\circ}$N,
         lon: $-12^{\circ}$--$15^{\circ}$E).
        Driven by Sahelian rainfall variability modulated by the
        West African Monsoon, high urban exposure in the lower Niger
        delta, and the longest individual flood records in the
        dataset (73 events; 5,617 total fatalities).

  \item \textit{East Africa --- Nile headwaters /
        Sudan--Ethiopia corridor}
        (lat: $-4^{\circ}$--$16^{\circ}$N,
         lon: $28^{\circ}$--$40^{\circ}$E).
        Characterised by seasonal Blue-Nile monsoon dynamics, large
        basin-routing distances, and the highest absolute exposure
        (96 events; 2,041 confirmed deaths and $1.5\times10^{7}$
        total affected in the 2023 Sudan event alone).

  \item \textit{Southern Africa --- Limpopo/Zambezi basin}
        (lat: $-27^{\circ}$--$-8^{\circ}$N,
         lon: $20^{\circ}$--$37^{\circ}$E).
        Subject to tropical cyclone forcing (Cyclone Idai 2019,
        Cyclone Freddy 2023), reservoir influence from Cahora Bassa,
        and drought--flood transitions (74 events; 544 deaths in the
        2022 Mozambique events).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Data Sources}
\label{sec:data}

The following observational datasets are combined to form the input
feature tensor and validation targets for \textsc{PADR-Net}:

\paragraph{Event catalogue.}
A harmonised flood-event table is constructed by joining the Global
Flood Database (GFD) satellite flood-extent records
\citep{Tellman2021} with the EM-DAT public disaster inventory
\citep{Guha-Sapir2016}.  The procedure follows
\citet{Kouadio2024}; only events with confirmed inundation
evidence are retained (243 events after quality control).

\paragraph{Meteorological forcing.}
ERA5 hourly reanalysis \citep{Hersbach2020} at $0.25^{\circ}$
horizontal resolution provides the precipitation time series
$P(x,y,t)$, near-surface air temperature $T_{2m}$, and soil
moisture.  The ERA5 event-window total precipitation
$P_{\mathrm{window}}$ (mm) is computed over a $\pm 10$-day window
centred on each flood event.

\paragraph{Terrain and bed-slope forcing.}
SRTM 30\,m elevation data \citep{Farr2007} is used to derive the
bed-elevation field $b(x,y)$ and the slope terms
$\partial b/\partial x$, $\partial b/\partial y$ in
$\mathbf{S}_{b}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:swe_sources}).  Positive-bias
correction for vegetation and buildings follows \citet{Hawker2022}.

\subsection{Feature Engineering and Model Inputs}
\label{sec:features}

For each flood event and sub-region, the input vector
$\mathbf{u}_{t} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{in}}}$ is assembled with
$N_{\mathrm{in}} = 12$ features:
\begin{itemize}
  \item ERA5 daily precipitation (5-day antecedent window: $P_{t},
        P_{t-1},\ldots,P_{t-4}$);
  \item ERA5 event-window total precipitation $P_{\mathrm{window}}$;
  \item ERA5 mean and maximum hourly precipitation during the event;
  \item near-surface temperature $T_{2m}$;
  \item SRTM terrain elevation $b$;
  \item bed-slope magnitude $\|\nabla b\|_{2}$;
  \item event duration in days;
  \item calendar month (proxy for seasonality / monsoon phase).
\end{itemize}

The reservoir size is set to $N_{\mathrm{res}} = 500$ neurons with
connectivity $p = 0.1$ (10\% non-zero entries in
$\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}$), giving a sparse reservoir matrix
consistent with biological neural network topology
\citep{Jaeger2004}.  The spectral radius is fixed at
$\rho(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{res}}) = 0.9$, satisfying the Echo State
condition of Lemma~\ref{lem:esp} with $\alpha = 0.9 < 1$.

\subsection{Validation Protocol and Ablation Study Design}
\label{sec:validation}

The 243 events are partitioned into:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Training set} (2000--2017; 120 events): moderate
        events with estimated return period $\leq 50$ years;
  \item \textbf{Validation set} (2018--2019; 7 events): used for
        hyperparameter tuning ($\lambda$, $N_{\mathrm{res}}$,
        regularisation);
  \item \textbf{Test set} (2020--2024; 116 events, of which 25 are
        classified as extreme): evaluated strictly out-of-sample.
\end{itemize}

The severity tier (moderate / extreme) is assigned by a composite
impact score $s = d + 0.001\,A + 0.01\,P_{\mathrm{window}}$, where
$d$ is event-confirmed deaths and $A$ is total affected; events with
$s \geq q_{90}$ of the training distribution are labelled
\emph{extreme} (proxy for $\geq 100$-year return period class).

Performance is assessed using three complementary metrics:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item Nash--Sutcliffe Efficiency $\mathrm{NSE} =
        1 - \Sigma_{t}(y_{t} - \hat{y}_{t})^{2} /
        \Sigma_{t}(y_{t} - \bar{y})^{2}$;
  \item Critical Success Index $\mathrm{CSI} =
        \mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP} + \mathrm{FP} + \mathrm{FN})$
        for binary inundation extent;
  \item Normalised cumulative mass error
        $\Delta M / M_{\mathrm{total}}$.
\end{enumerate}

The \textbf{ablation study} contrasts two configurations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PADR-Net-0} ($\lambda = 0$): unconstrained DRN
        baseline (standard Echo State Network); and
  \item \textbf{PADR-Net-$\lambda$} ($\lambda > 0$): full
        physics-constrained framework with optimal $\lambda$
        selected on the validation set.
\end{itemize}
Both models share identical reservoir architecture and are
trained on the same 120-event training set, ensuring that any
performance difference is attributable solely to the physical
constraint, as predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_error}.
